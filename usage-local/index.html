iOS on-device monitor

Track AI spend without sending a byte to anyone.

Usage Local Monitor is the no-account counterpart to its server-backed sibling. You enter your plan and your daily spend manually; the app rolls it up locally and warns you before you hit the cap. There is no server, no cloud sync, no account. Your numbers stay on your phone.

What's inside

  • Caps and daily spend. Set a per-provider cap; log your daily spend; the app warns when you're approaching the line.
  • Model availability. Per-provider, per-model -- a quick lookup of which models are accessible on your plan, with quota brackets where the provider publishes them.
  • Manual entry, no scraping. The app reads no usage endpoints; you stay in control of what enters it.
  • Local notifications. Cap-warning notifications fire from your device, not a server. No background telemetry, no relay.
  • CSV export. Per-provider, per-date-range. Export once, archive forever.

How it works

Usage Local Monitor is the simplest thing that works: a local store for plan, cap, and the day's spend. There is no provider integration, no API key, no account. The app never talks to a server. The trade-off is that you maintain the entry -- but the upside is that nothing leaves the device.

  1. Add your plan and per-provider cap in the Providers screen.
  2. Log your daily spend; the app rolls it up locally.
  3. Set a cap-warning threshold and let the local notification fire when you're close.

What it doesn't do

  • Provider integration. The app does not read any provider's usage endpoint. You enter your spend manually.
  • Cross-device sync. No iCloud, no server. If you wipe the device, your numbers go with it (CSV export first).
  • Auto-renewal tracking. The app does not manage subscriptions or trial expiry.

Privacy

Usage Local Monitor runs entirely on-device. No account, no server, no cloud sync, no analytics. The app does not transmit your spend to anyone. Crash reports are opt-in and scrubbed of identifiers. Full notice at /privacy.

Need help?

The most common hiccup is forgetting the manual entry after a long session. The support page covers the four checks that catch 90% of "the cap warning never fires" reports; if those don't help, write to feedback@simplewithus.com with your iOS version.
