iOS orientation rules

Lock or rotate orientation exactly when you need it.

Autorotate.Codes is the iOS app for orientation that respects context. Lock Instagram to portrait. Force YouTube landscape when a specific Bluetooth speaker connects. Pin a reading app to portrait when you prop the phone in a desk cradle. The rules you want, when you want them, without the fight with iOS defaults.

What's inside

  • Per-app rules. Pick an app, pick a locked or forced orientation. The rule takes effect the next time the app comes to the foreground.
  • Per-URL rules. Inside supported browsers, match on URL pattern. Some sites assume landscape; some force their own layout regardless.
  • Bluetooth triggers. Pair a device -- a car mount, a gym cradle, a desk dock -- and the rule fires when it connects.
  • Schedule rules. Time-of-day rules for the times your context shifts. Work hours portrait, evening unlocked.
  • macOS counterpart. The Mac sibling handles display-rotation rules. Rules can roam between devices with iCloud Drive.

How it works

Autorotate.Codes hooks into iOS orientation events through the standard app-foreground lifecycle. The app applies your rule when an app comes forward, when a URL pattern matches, when a paired Bluetooth device connects, or when the schedule fires. There is no jailbreak, no private API; the app respects the limits iOS exposes.

  1. Open the Rules screen and add your first rule. Pick the trigger (app, URL, device, schedule) and the orientation.
  2. Test by opening the targeted app or visiting the targeted URL.
  3. Tune. The Diagnostics screen shows recent rule firings and skipped events.

What it doesn't do

  • Force apps that ignore orientation. Some apps (most video players, most camera apps) take over orientation lock at the app level. The rule fires; the app ignores it. We surface the rule fire in Diagnostics; the override is the app's, not ours.
  • iPad split-view per-window. iPadOS exposes per-window orientation; that comes in a later release.
  • System-wide rotation without a rule. Every action requires an explicit rule. The app never changes orientation behind your back.

Privacy

Autorotate.Codes only needs to manage your orientation rules. No telemetry, no account, no analytics. Rules sync through iCloud Drive if you opt in; otherwise they stay on-device. Crash reports are opt-in. Full notice at /privacy.

Need help?

If a rule fires but the app ignores it, that's almost always the app, not the rule. The support page covers the four checks that catch 90% of "rule did nothing" reports; if those don't help, write to feedback@simplewithus.com with your iOS version and the app you were testing.
